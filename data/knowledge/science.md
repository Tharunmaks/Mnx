# Science

## The solar system

The solar system has eight planets. In order from the Sun they are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.
Jupiter is the largest planet in the solar system.
Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest planet to the Sun.
Venus is the hottest planet because its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere traps heat.
Mars is called the Red Planet because iron oxide (rust) on its surface makes it look red.
Saturn is famous for its bright ring system made of ice and rock.
Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet by the International Astronomical Union in 2006.
The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite and is about 384,400 km from Earth on average.
The Sun is a star made mostly of hydrogen and helium; its light takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth.
Earth is about 150 million kilometres (1 astronomical unit) from the Sun.
Earth takes about 365.25 days to orbit the Sun and about 24 hours to rotate once on its axis.

## Physics

The speed of light in a vacuum is exactly 299,792,458 metres per second.
Gravity accelerates objects near Earth's surface at about 9.81 metres per second squared.
Isaac Newton described the three laws of motion and the law of universal gravitation in 1687.
Albert Einstein published the theory of special relativity in 1905 and general relativity in 1915.
The equation E = mc² states that energy equals mass times the speed of light squared.
Sound travels through air at about 343 metres per second at 20 °C.
Absolute zero, 0 kelvin or -273.15 °C, is the lowest possible temperature.
An atom consists of a nucleus of protons and neutrons surrounded by electrons.
Electric current is measured in amperes, voltage in volts and resistance in ohms.
Ohm's law states that voltage equals current times resistance (V = IR).

## Chemistry

Water has the chemical formula H2O: two hydrogen atoms bonded to one oxygen atom.
At sea level water freezes at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C.
The periodic table arranges the chemical elements by atomic number.
Hydrogen has the chemical symbol H and atomic number 1; it is the lightest and most abundant element in the universe.
Helium has the chemical symbol He and atomic number 2.
Lithium has the chemical symbol Li and atomic number 3.
Carbon has the chemical symbol C and atomic number 6.
Nitrogen has the chemical symbol N and atomic number 7; it makes up about 78% of Earth's atmosphere.
Oxygen has the chemical symbol O and atomic number 8; it makes up about 21% of Earth's atmosphere.
Sodium has the chemical symbol Na and atomic number 11.
Iron has the chemical symbol Fe and atomic number 26.
Gold has the chemical symbol Au and atomic number 79.
Silver has the chemical symbol Ag and atomic number 47.
Table salt is sodium chloride, NaCl.
Carbon dioxide, CO2, is a greenhouse gas released by burning fossil fuels.
A pH of 7 is neutral; lower values are acidic and higher values are basic.

## Biology

DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) carries the genetic instructions of living organisms.
James Watson and Francis Crick described the double-helix structure of DNA in 1953, using X-ray data from Rosalind Franklin.
Photosynthesis is the process by which plants use sunlight, water and carbon dioxide to make glucose and release oxygen.
The mitochondrion is often called the powerhouse of the cell because it produces most of the cell's energy (ATP).
Charles Darwin proposed the theory of evolution by natural selection in On the Origin of Species (1859).
An adult human skeleton has 206 bones.
The human heart has four chambers: two atria and two ventricles.
Red blood cells carry oxygen using the protein haemoglobin.
The largest organ of the human body is the skin.
The blue whale is the largest animal known to have ever lived.
Penicillin, the first widely used antibiotic, was discovered by Alexander Fleming in 1928.
